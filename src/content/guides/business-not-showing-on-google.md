The most common call I get starts like this: "I Googled my own business and I'm not there. What did I do wrong?"

Usually, nothing dramatic. One time it was a family-owned bakery in Escondido. Great reviews on Yelp, lines out the door on Saturdays, and completely invisible on Google Maps. The owner was convinced Google had something against her. The actual problem? Her Google Business Profile had never been verified. It had been sitting there, half set up, for three years. We fixed it in an afternoon.

That's the good news about not showing up on Google: it's almost always one of a handful of fixable problems. Here are the 12 I see most, starting with the 5-minute fixes.

## The 5-minute fixes

### 1. You don't have a Google Business Profile (or someone else claimed it)

Search your business name on Google Maps. If nothing comes up, you need to create a profile at business.google.com. If something comes up but you can't edit it, someone else owns it, often a former employee or an old marketing company. Google has a process to request ownership. Start it today, because it can take a while.

### 2. Your profile isn't verified

An unverified profile is like a store with the lights off. Google won't show it much, if at all. Log in and check for a "Verify" button. Verification might be a video, a phone call, or a postcard, depending on what Google asks for.

### 3. Your profile was suspended

If you log in and see a red warning, your profile was suspended. The most common reasons I see:

- Extra keywords in the business name ("Mike's Plumbing Best Plumber San Diego")
- Using a P.O. box or virtual office as your address
- Showing a home address when you should be set up as a service-area business

Fix the problem first, then submit a reinstatement request. Don't create a second profile. That makes things worse.

### 4. You picked the wrong primary category

Your primary category is one of the strongest signals Google uses. I once worked with a med spa listed as "Beauty Salon." They were competing against hair salons for searches they didn't even want. Changing it to the right category moved them onto the map for "Botox near me" within a few weeks.

Pick the most specific category that matches your main service. Add secondary categories for the rest.

### 5. You (or someone) made a big recent edit

Changing your name, address, or category can trigger a re-review, and your profile may drop while Google checks it. If you just made changes, give it a couple of weeks before you panic.

## The fixes that take a little longer

### 6. You don't have enough reviews (or recent ones)

Reviews are one of the biggest reasons one business shows up and another doesn't. If your competitors have 150 reviews and you have 9, that's a big part of your answer. Recent reviews matter too. A profile with no new reviews in a year looks closed, to Google and to customers.

How to fix it without being awkward: [How to Get More Google Reviews](/how-to-get-more-google-reviews).

### 7. Your name, address, and phone don't match across the internet

If Google says Suite 200, your website says Suite 210, and Yelp has your old phone number, Google gets less confident about which one is right. Pick one exact version and make it match everywhere: your website, Google, Apple Maps, Bing, Facebook, Yelp, and industry directories.

### 8. Your website doesn't say what you do or where you do it

Google reads your website to figure out what you're about. A homepage that says "Quality You Can Trust" doesn't tell Google you're an HVAC company in Chula Vista. Say it plainly, in words: what you do, where you do it, and who you do it for.

### 9. Your website is slow or broken on phones

Most local searches happen on a phone. If your site takes forever to load or the "Call" button doesn't work, people leave, and Google notices. Open your site on your own phone, on cellular data, and see how it feels.

### 10. You're outside the area people are searching from

Google weighs distance. If you're 20 miles from where most of your customers live, you'll struggle to show up for "near me" searches there. You can't move your shop, but you can build location pages for the areas you serve and earn reviews from customers in those areas.

### 11. Your competitors are simply doing more

Sometimes nothing is wrong. Your competitors just have more reviews, better photos, more active profiles, and stronger websites. That's not a penalty. It's a to-do list.

### 12. You're not showing up in AI answers, either

More customers now ask ChatGPT or Google's AI for recommendations instead of scrolling the map. Those tools lean heavily on your reviews, your listings, and what other sites say about you. If you're invisible on Google, you're probably invisible there too. More on that in [How to Get Your Business Recommended by ChatGPT](/get-business-recommended-by-chatgpt).

## Why you show in Google search but not on Maps (and vice versa)

These are two different systems, and they fail for different reasons.

- **Not on Maps, but you show in regular search:** Maps is powered by your Google Business Profile. If the profile is missing, unverified, suspended, or placed in the wrong category, you won't appear on the map no matter how good your website is.
- **On Maps, but not in regular search:** your profile is fine, but your website isn't being trusted for the search. This is usually a thin homepage, missing service pages, or weak signals from other sites. Fix #8 and Fix #11 cover most of it.
- **Nowhere at all:** start with the profile. It's the faster of the two to fix and it feeds both.

Knowing which of the three you are tells you where to start. Most owners jump straight to the website when the real problem is the profile.

## Before you panic: four things to check first

Half the "my business disappeared" emergencies I get are false alarms. Check these before changing anything:

1. **Search in an incognito window.** Your browser's saved history and location can show you your own business where customers would never see it.
2. **Search from a different device, on cell data.** Google personalizes results by location. If you work from home, you may be seeing a version of Google your customers never see.
3. **Search the exact name on your profile, word for word.** If your sign says "Rivera's Plumbing" but your profile says "Rivera Plumbing & Drain Services," customers searching the sign version may not find you.
4. **Check the date of your last edit.** If you changed your name, address, or category in the last couple of weeks, Google may be re-reviewing your profile. This usually resolves itself; making more changes makes it worse.

If it's still missing after all four, work through the 12 fixes in order. The order matters: the first five are quick and fix the most common causes.

## What not to do when you're invisible

- **Don't create a second profile.** Duplicates split your reviews and can get both suspended.
- **Don't buy reviews or review services.** Google removes them, and sudden fake review spikes are one of the fastest ways to get suspended.
- **Don't keyword-stuff your business name** ("Joe's Plumbing Best Plumber San Diego"). It violates Google's guidelines and can get the profile suspended or renamed.
- **Don't panic-edit everything at once.** Batch changes look suspicious. Fix one thing, wait, observe.
- **Don't hire anyone who promises a specific ranking date.** Nobody can promise that. If they do, that's your answer about whether to trust them.

## FAQ

**Why is my business on Google but not on Google Maps?**
Usually your Google Business Profile is missing, unverified, or suspended. A website alone can show up in regular search results, but you need a verified profile to show up on the map.

**How long does it take to show up on Google after verifying?**
Often a few days, sometimes a few weeks. Ranking higher on the map takes longer and depends on reviews, category, and competition.

**Can I pay Google to show up on the map?**
You can run Google Ads, including ads that appear on Maps. But you can't pay to rank higher in the free results. Those have to be earned.

**Does it matter if I work from home?**
Yes. If customers don't come to you, set up your profile as a service-area business and hide your home address. Showing a home address when you shouldn't is a common reason for suspensions.

**Why is my business not showing up on Google Maps?**
The map is powered by your Google Business Profile, not your website. The usual causes are a missing profile, an unverified one, a suspension, or the wrong primary category. A perfect website won't put you on the map; a healthy profile will.

**Why does my business show on Maps but not in Google search?**
Your profile is doing its job, but Google doesn't trust your website enough to show it in the main results. That usually means a thin homepage, no pages that match what people search for, or almost no other sites mentioning you. Fixes 8, 9, and 11 are where to start.

**I just verified my profile. Why can't I see it yet?**
Verification is the start, not the finish. A brand-new profile can take days to a few weeks to appear in Maps and longer to rank. Also check it in an incognito window from a different device — you may already be live and just seeing a personalized view.

**Why did my business suddenly disappear from Google?**
The most common causes, in order: a recent edit triggered a re-review, the profile got suspended (often for a guideline violation like keyword-stuffing the business name), someone else claimed or reported the listing, or you're searching in a way that hides it (logged in, home address). Check each before changing anything.

**Can I show up on Google without a website?**
Yes, for Maps — a complete, verified profile with reviews can rank on the map with no website at all. But you'll struggle in regular search results, and AI tools like ChatGPT lean heavily on websites. A simple one-page site beats no site.
