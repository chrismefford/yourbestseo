A landscaper in El Cajon once showed me his Google Ads bill. He was spending about $2,400 a month. When I asked what happened the month he paused it, he laughed and said, "The phone stopped ringing. So I turned it back on."

That's the trap. Ads work, but only while you're paying. The second you stop, you disappear. I call it renting your customers.

I'm not anti-ads. I've run plenty of them. But most small businesses I talk to don't want to quit ads cold turkey. They want to need them less. Here's how.

## First, the math that keeps owners up at night

According to WordStream's 2026 benchmarks, the average Google Ads click costs $5.42, and the average cost to get a lead is $66.69. That's not a customer. That's someone who filled out a form or called. Some of them will never buy.

So if you need 30 leads a month, you're looking at roughly $2,000 a month, every month, forever. Now imagine putting even half of that into things you own.

### 1. Your Google Business Profile

This is the single best free marketing tool most small businesses have and ignore. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 85% of small businesses had a Google Business Profile, but only 35% kept it active. Fill it out completely, post updates, add photos every month, and answer every review. Use the [Google Business Profile Checklist](/google-business-profile-checklist).

### 2. Reviews

Reviews do two jobs: they help you rank, and they help people choose you. In BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey, 97% of consumers said they read reviews for local businesses. Build a simple habit for asking. [Here's how to do it without being awkward](/how-to-get-more-google-reviews).

### 3. Pages that answer what people search

Every question a customer asks you on the phone is a page you should have on your website. "How much does a water heater replacement cost in San Diego?" "Do I need a permit for a fence?" Answer it plainly, and you'll start showing up for those searches.

That landscaper? We wrote eight pages answering the questions his customers asked most: drought-friendly yards, artificial turf costs, irrigation repair. Six months later, organic search was bringing in more calls than his ads. He cut his ad budget in half and kept it there.

### 4. Getting listed everywhere that matters

Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp, your Chamber of Commerce, industry directories. Each listing is another place customers can find you and another signal to Google that you're real.

### 5. Showing up in AI answers

This one's new and growing fast. In BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey, 45% of consumers said they'd used ChatGPT or other AI tools to find local businesses, up from 6% the year before. Those tools pull from your reviews, listings, and website. [Here's how to show up](/get-business-recommended-by-chatgpt).

### 6. An email list

Your past customers are your cheapest new customers. A simple monthly email with a seasonal tip and a reminder you exist will bring people back for years.

### 7. Referral partners

The plumber knows the remodeler. The dog groomer knows the vet. Make a list of five businesses that serve your same customers and buy one of them lunch this month.

## When ads still make sense

I'd be lying if I said you should never run ads. They're great when:

- You're brand new and need customers before SEO kicks in
- You have a seasonal rush and need to fill the calendar fast
- You're launching a new service and want to test demand

The goal isn't zero ads. It's ads as a choice, not a life support system. For a straight comparison, read [SEO vs. Google Ads](/seo-vs-google-ads).

## A realistic plan for the next 90 days

**Month 1:** Fix your Google Business Profile. Start asking every happy customer for a review. Make sure your info matches everywhere.

**Month 2:** Write three pages answering your most-asked customer questions. Get listed on five more directories.

**Month 3:** Check where your calls are coming from. If organic calls are climbing, trim your ad budget by 10 to 20% and watch what happens.

Then repeat. Slowly, the balance shifts from rented to owned.

## FAQ

**Can a small business really get customers without ads?**
Yes. Plenty do it entirely through their Google profile, reviews, website, and referrals. It takes longer than ads to start, but it keeps working when you stop paying.

**What's the best free way to get customers online?**
For most local businesses, it's a complete, active Google Business Profile with a steady stream of reviews.

**Should I stop my Google Ads right now?**
Probably not all at once. Build your free channels first, then reduce ads gradually while you watch your call volume.
