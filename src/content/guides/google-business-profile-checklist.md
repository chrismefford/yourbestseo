If I could make every small business owner do one thing, it would be this checklist.

Your Google Business Profile is the little box that shows up on Google Maps and on the right side of Google when someone searches your name. It's free. It's often the first thing a customer sees. And most businesses set it up once and never touch it again.

In BrightLocal's 2026 survey of small business owners, 85% had a Google Business Profile, but only 35% kept it active. That gap is the opportunity. An auto shop I worked with in Vista went from showing up on page two of the map to the top three for "brake repair near me" in about two months. We didn't run a single ad. We just worked through this list.

## Before you start

- [ ] Log in at business.google.com with the Google account that owns your profile
- [ ] If you don't know who owns it, figure that out first. A former employee or old marketing company often still has access.
- [ ] Have your exact business name, address, phone, hours, and website URL ready, written the same way you'll use everywhere

## The basics

- [ ] **Business name** exactly as it appears on your sign. No extra keywords. "Vista Auto Care," not "Vista Auto Care Best Brake Repair Vista CA."
- [ ] **Profile is verified.** If you see a "Verify" button, do it now.
- [ ] **Primary category** is the most specific one that matches your main service
- [ ] **Secondary categories** added for your other main services
- [ ] **Address** matches your website exactly, down to the suite number. If customers don't come to you, set up as a service-area business and hide your address.
- [ ] **Service area** listed by city or zip code, if you travel to customers
- [ ] **Phone number** is one someone actually answers
- [ ] **Website link** points to your most useful page, not just your homepage, if a better page exists

## Hours

- [ ] Regular hours are accurate
- [ ] **Special hours** set for holidays, and set them a few weeks ahead
- [ ] Extra hours added if you have them (delivery, pickup, senior hours, and so on)

Nothing makes a customer angrier than driving to a closed store that Google said was open.

## Description and details

- [ ] **Description** written in plain language: what you do, who you help, where, and what makes you different. Use the full space.
- [ ] **Services or products** listed, with short descriptions and prices if you can share them
- [ ] **Attributes** filled in (wheelchair accessible, women-owned, veteran-owned, free Wi-Fi, appointments required, and so on)
- [ ] **Opening date** added

## Photos

- [ ] **Logo** uploaded
- [ ] **Cover photo** that shows your business with people in it, not a stock image
- [ ] **Outside photo** showing your storefront or entrance, so people recognize it when they arrive
- [ ] **Inside photos** of your space
- [ ] **Team photos** with real faces
- [ ] **Work photos**: before-and-afters, finished jobs, products
- [ ] New photos added at least once a month

## Reviews

- [ ] A simple system for asking every happy customer for a review (text or email works best)
- [ ] Every review gets a reply, good or bad, within a few days
- [ ] Negative reviews answered calmly, without arguing, with an offer to make it right
- [ ] No incentives offered for reviews. Google doesn't allow it.

## Posts and updates

- [ ] Post something at least twice a month: offers, events, seasonal tips, new services, finished projects
- [ ] Add an event or offer post when you have one

## The big 2026 change: AI answers

Google removed the public Questions and Answers section from Business Profiles in late 2025. In its place, Google's AI now answers people's questions about your business by pulling from your profile, your reviews, your website, and other mentions of you online.

That means you can't type your own answers anymore. Your job now is to make sure the AI has accurate material:

- [ ] Your description, services, and attributes cover the questions people ask most
- [ ] Your website answers common questions plainly, like parking, pricing, and whether you take walk-ins
- [ ] Your reviews describe real experiences (encourage specifics)

## Working from home? Read this before you publish

If customers come to you (an office, a shop, a studio), your address should be visible — it helps you rank for "near me" searches.

If customers don't come to you (plumbing, house cleaning, mobile notary, lawn care), you're a **service-area business**, and the setup is different:

1. **Hide your address.** Google will show it as "Serves [area]" instead of your home.
2. **List your service areas.** You can add up to 20 areas (cities or zip codes). Keep them real — areas where you'll genuinely drive to.
3. **Don't create a profile for every city you serve.** One profile with multiple service areas. Multiple profiles for one business at one address get flagged as duplicates.

Getting this wrong is one of the most common causes of suspensions, and it's completely avoidable.

## The suspension causes you can prevent

Most suspensions come from a short list of mistakes:

- **Keyword-stuffing the business name.** "Joe's Plumbing — Best Plumber San Diego 24/7" is against the rules. Use your real name, the one on your storefront and license.
- **A virtual office or mailbox address** you don't actually operate from.
- **Multiple profiles** for the same business at the same address.
- **Someone else managing the profile who's also managing your competitors.** Google treats this as a conflict; you can be affected by what they do.
- **Big sudden changes** — renaming, recategorizing, or moving the pin all at once.

If you do get suspended, appeal from the profile itself and be ready to prove you're a real business at that address: a license, utility bill, or photos of signage. It usually takes a week or two. Don't create a new profile while you wait — that makes it worse.

## What your competitors' profiles tell you (10-minute exercise)

Find the top three businesses ranking on the map for your main service. Open each profile and write down:

- How many reviews they have, and how recent the newest one is
- How many photos, and whether any are real (not stock)
- Whether they post updates, and how often
- What categories they use
- Whether their description names their main service

You now have a target list. You don't need to beat them everywhere — but any category where you're at zero is a gap Google can see.

## FAQ

**Is a Google Business Profile really free?**
Yes. Creating and managing it costs nothing. Be skeptical of anyone who calls claiming you need to pay Google to keep your listing.

**How often should I update my Google Business Profile?**
At least monthly: new photos, a couple of posts, and replies to reviews. Update hours any time they change.

**Why did my Google Business Profile get suspended?**
The most common reasons are keywords in the business name, using a P.O. box or virtual office address, or showing a home address when you should be a service-area business. Fix the issue, then request reinstatement.

**Does my Google Business Profile affect ChatGPT and AI search?**
Indirectly, yes. AI tools draw on local listings and reviews, and Google's own AI uses your profile directly to answer questions about your business.

**Can I have more than one location?**
Yes — each real, staffed location that customers can visit should have its own profile, and each one needs its own reviews and photos. One location means one profile. Extra profiles for places you don't actually operate from will get flagged.

**Should I hide my address if I work from home?**
If customers never come to your home, yes. Set it up as a service-area business: hide the address, list the areas you serve. Showing your home address when customers shouldn't visit is a common and avoidable suspension cause.

**What photos actually help?**
Real photos of your storefront, team, work in progress, and finished jobs. Google and customers both respond to them, and customers are more likely to click a profile with real photos. Skip stock images — people can tell, and Google has gotten good at it too.

**How do I get my review link?**
From your profile, there's a "share review form" or "ask for reviews" option that gives you a short link you can text or email. Save it in your phone so you can send it in ten seconds at the happy moment. More on the exact scripts in [How to Get More Google Reviews](/how-to-get-more-google-reviews).

**What if someone else already claimed my business?**
You can request access through the profile — Google will have the current owner verify or deny, and if they don't respond, you can claim it with proof (license, signage photos, business registration). Don't give up if your first request is ignored; the process favors the real business.
