I'm in the SEO business, and I'm about to tell you about the worst parts of it.

A big chunk of my new clients come to me after getting burned. A chiropractor in Encinitas paid $900 a month for two years to a company that, as far as we could tell, did nothing. When she tried to leave, they wouldn't give her access to her own Google Business Profile. That's the moment that sticks with me. Not because it's rare, but because it's common.

Here are the nine scams and shady practices I see most, and how to protect yourself.

## 1. "We guarantee #1 on Google"

Nobody can guarantee rankings. Google's own guidance on hiring an SEO says no one can guarantee a #1 spot. Anyone who promises it is either lying or planning to rank you for a search nobody actually makes, like "best chiropractor on Elm Street with blue doors."

**Ask:** "Which exact searches will I rank for, and how many people search for them each month?"

## 2. The "Google partner" phone call

You get a call: "This is Google. Your listing is about to be removed unless you verify it with us today." It's not Google. Google doesn't call small businesses to threaten their listing, and managing your Google Business Profile is free.

**Do:** Hang up. Log in to your own profile at business.google.com and check it yourself.

## 3. They own your accounts

Your Google Business Profile, website, domain name, and analytics should be owned by you. Some companies set everything up under their own accounts, so when you leave, you lose it all. That's what happened to the chiropractor.

**Ask:** "Will I be the owner of every account, with you added as a manager?" The only acceptable answer is yes.

## 4. Long contracts with no exit

A 12- or 24-month contract with no way out isn't a sign of commitment. It's a sign they don't expect you to want to stay.

**Ask:** "What happens if I want to cancel after three months?"

## 5. Secret "proprietary" methods

If they can't explain what they're doing in plain English, that's a problem. Good SEO isn't magic. It's a lot of unglamorous work: fixing listings, building reviews, writing helpful pages, fixing technical problems.

**Ask:** "What exactly will you do in the first 90 days?"

## 6. Reports full of numbers that don't matter

"Impressions are up 400%!" Great. Is the phone ringing? Some companies send long reports full of vanity metrics to hide the fact that nothing is changing for your business.

**Ask:** "Will you show me calls, form fills, and direction requests, not just traffic?"

## 7. Buying links or fake reviews

Cheap services sometimes buy spammy links or fake reviews to get quick bumps. It can work briefly, then backfire badly, including penalties, removed reviews, or a suspended Google profile.

**Ask:** "How do you get links and reviews?" If the answer is vague or involves "our network," walk away.

## 8. Thousands of junk pages

Some companies pump out hundreds of near-identical pages, one for every town in the county, with your business name swapped in. It's lazy, it rarely works anymore, and it can hurt your site.

**Ask:** "Can I see examples of pages you've written for other clients?"

## 9. "AI SEO" that's just the old stuff with a new label

AI search is real, and it matters. In BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey, 45% of consumers used AI tools to find local businesses. But some companies are slapping "AI" on the same old packages and charging more. Real AI visibility work focuses on reviews, consistent listings, community mentions, and clear answers on your website.

**Ask:** "Show me what ChatGPT says about my business today, and what you'll change."

## The 10 questions to ask any SEO before you hire them

1. Which specific searches will you target, and how many people make them?
2. Will I own every account?
3. What will you do in the first 90 days?
4. How will you report results? What counts as success?
5. How do you get links and reviews?
6. Can I see work you've done for businesses like mine?
7. Can I talk to two current clients?
8. What's the contract length, and how do I cancel?
9. What can I do myself to save money?
10. When should I expect to see results? (Be wary of anything faster than a few months for real growth.)

Question 9 is my favorite. A good SEO will happily tell you what you can do yourself. A scammer will tell you it's too complicated.

## FAQ

**How do I know if my SEO company is legit?**
They explain what they're doing in plain language, report on real business results, let you own your accounts, and don't guarantee rankings.

**Does Google call businesses about their listing?**
Google doesn't call to threaten removal or demand payment to keep your listing. If you get a call like that, hang up and check your profile yourself.

**I think I got scammed. What should I do?**
First, make sure you have owner access to your Google Business Profile, website, domain, and analytics. Then get a second opinion on what was actually done.
