You'd expect an SEO guy to tell you SEO always wins. I'm not going to, because it doesn't.

I've watched a brand-new food truck owner sink money into SEO when what she needed was a weekend of ads to get people in line. And I've watched an established roofing company spend $4,000 a month on ads for searches they could have owned for free. The right answer depends on where your business is right now.

## The quick comparison

| | Google Ads | SEO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **How fast it works** | Same day | Usually 3 to 6 months for real traction |
| **What you pay for** | Every click | Time or expertise to build it |
| **What happens when you stop paying** | Leads stop | Results keep going for a while |
| **Average cost** | About $5.42 per click and $66.69 per lead (WordStream, 2026) | Often $500 to $3,000 a month if you hire help, or your time if you don't |
| **Trust from customers** | Some people skip ads | Organic results and reviews tend to feel more trustworthy |
| **Best for** | Speed, testing, seasonal spikes | Long-term, steady leads you don't rent |

## When Google Ads is the right call

- **You're new.** SEO takes months to build. Ads can bring customers in this week.
- **You have a short-term push.** A grand opening, a seasonal rush, a sale.
- **You're testing a new service.** Ads tell you fast whether people want it.
- **Your market is tiny and specific.** Sometimes a small, tightly targeted ad budget is cheaper than the work SEO would take.

The food truck owner? We ran a small, location-targeted ad campaign for her first two months while she built up reviews. Once she had steady regulars and 60+ reviews, she dropped the ads entirely.

## When SEO is the right call

- **You plan to be around for years.** SEO is an investment that compounds.
- **People search for what you do.** Plumbers, dentists, lawyers, HVAC, salons. If people search "[your service] near me," SEO matters.
- **Your ad costs keep climbing.** If you're paying $50+ per lead, every organic lead is real money saved.
- **You want to show up in AI answers too.** The same things that help SEO, like reviews, listings, and helpful content, are what ChatGPT and Google's AI use to recommend businesses.

That roofing company cut its ad spend by about 60% over a year as organic calls picked up. They still run ads during storm season, when everybody needs a roofer at once. That's the smart version: ads as a tool, not a dependency.

## The answer for most small businesses: both, then shift

For most of the local businesses I work with, the best plan looks like this:

1. **Start with the free stuff.** Fix your [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile-checklist) and start collecting [reviews](/how-to-get-more-google-reviews). This costs nothing and helps both ads and SEO.
2. **Use ads for speed** while you build.
3. **Build SEO steadily** with pages that answer your customers' real questions.
4. **Shift the budget over time.** As organic leads grow, trim ads gradually and watch the phone.

The goal is a business that doesn't panic when the ad budget pauses. More on that in [How to Get Customers Without Paying for Google Ads](/get-customers-without-google-ads).

## Questions to ask yourself

- If I turned off ads tomorrow, how many calls would I lose?
- What am I paying per customer from ads right now, not per click?
- How many of my customers say they found me on Google, but not through an ad?
- Do I need customers in the next 30 days, or am I building for next year?

Your answers will tell you more than any blog post, including this one.

## FAQ

**Is Google Ads worth it for a small business?**
It can be, especially for new businesses, seasonal pushes, and testing. It gets expensive as a permanent strategy because results stop when spending stops.

**Is SEO better than Google Ads?**
Neither is always better. Ads are faster. SEO lasts longer and costs less per lead over time. Most businesses do best using ads for speed while building SEO for the long run.

**Can I do both?**
Yes, and most businesses should, at least for a while. They support each other.

**How long until SEO can replace my ads?**
It depends on your market. Many local businesses see meaningful organic growth in 3 to 6 months and can start trimming ads within a year. See [How Long Does SEO Take?](/how-long-does-seo-take)
